Crownguard · tower concepts · round 1

The towers, in the new style

You asked for the towers themselves to get the same lift as the people on them. Here are three of them (the Archer hall, the Wizard Spire and the Catapult), each drawn three ways beside the one in the game today. Nothing in the game has changed yet. Pick a letter and I build the rest to match.

Today, and where it could go

Left: the Archer tower in the game right now, with the new archers already standing on it. Right: the same tower redrawn (treatment B).

The new people are drawn with a thin outline in each material's own dark colour, lit by one sun from the upper left. Today's towers still carry the older, heavier black outline and flatter shading, so new people on old towers look like two different games. That is the first thing every option below fixes.

  • Line. The heavy black ring becomes the same thin tinted line the figures have. Stone joints stop being black lines and become lighter and darker stones.
  • Light. One sun. Each tower gets a lit side, a shaded side and a little bounce light on its far edge, so it reads as round. The crown throws a real shadow onto the wall under it.
  • Materials. Stone, timber, slate, iron and gilt each get their own five shades, so they stop looking like the same thing in different colours.
  • Life. Flags ripple, torches flicker and light the wall beside them, windows glow, and a spell lights the stone as it is cast.

One honest limit, the same one the figures had: the board has no spare pixels. A tower cannot get more detailed than this at battle size. The gain is better pixels and more life, not more of them.

Growing with each level

At battle size

About the size an iPad shows them. Columns, left to right: today, A, B, C.

This is the view that matters most, and two honest things show in it. At this size A and B are closer than they look enlarged: B's extra work reads as texture and warmth more than as separate stones. And the thin line is softer against the grass than today's black ring, exactly as it was for the figures you approved; the towers hold their shape through light and shadow instead.

What it costs in game speed

Measured just now on the device you are holding.

…Measuring.

A tower is drawn once, the first time it appears, and kept as a picture. After that it is a stamp, exactly as today. The moving parts (a flag, a flame, a patch of light) are small pictures made ahead of time and stamped on top. So all three options run at about the speed the towers run at now. The only new cost is the first drawing of each kind of tower, which the game can do quietly in spare moments, as it already does for the new figures.

What it costs in time
