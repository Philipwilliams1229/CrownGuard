The sunset before the Blood Moon: four options

Bellmarsh, a wave in progress, drawn by the real game. Each strip steps the setting sun through seven moments (k 0 to 1, left to right, then the second row), ending in the blood moon's night. The sun sets off the left, behind the wood; the red moonlight follows from the upper left as it does today. Below each strip, a 2x close-up of a hall, the road and some foes at k 0.45.

Mock-ups for choosing: the castle's gate torches are faked in B; crows and water in D are concept sketches.

Today (for comparison)

One orange wash over the whole board that swells and fades. This is the one that read like a faulty screen.

A. The dusk line

A ragged, treeline-shaped shadow edge sweeps from the upper left to the lower right. Behind it the night's plum shade is already at full strength; ahead of it is plain daylight, with a thin band of gold dither on the last sunlit ground. The red light and the dead's eyes arrive once the line has passed.

B. A, and the lamps light as the line passes

Everything in A, and each hall's torch flares and then pools as the shadow crosses it, the dead's eyes ignite the same way foe by foe, and the castle's gate torches are the last to catch.

C. Long shadows and a gold rim, then the night

No edge and no tint: first every hall and figure throws a long shadow down and to the right and catches an amber rim on its upper-left edge; then the night fades in evenly behind them (a plain fade, chosen because the point of this option is that nothing sweeps).

D. A, and the water carries the sky, with crows

The dusk line of A, plus rivers and ponds that glint gold ahead of the line, turn rose at it and go blood-red once the moon is up, and a flight of crows crosses just ahead of the line, each with a shadow on the ground.
